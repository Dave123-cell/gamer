Reference Formatting

Citation Styles Reference

Every major academic citation style used by leading journals, with formatted examples to guide your conversion.

Nature Style (Nature family, PNAS, PLOS)
Numbered; superscript or inline; minimal author info
1. Smith, J. A., Jones, B. & Williams, C. K. Article title in sentence case. Nature 592, 41–47 (2021). https://doi.org/xxx
Vancouver / NLM (NEJM, Lancet, BMJ, JAMA)
Numbered in order of appearance; abbreviated journal names
1. Smith JA, Jones B, Williams CK. Article title. N Engl J Med. 2021;384(8):701-11. doi:10.1056/NEJMxxx
APA 7th Edition (Psychology, Social Sciences)
Author-date; hanging indent; DOI required when available
Smith, J. A., Jones, B., & Williams, C. K. (2021). Article title in sentence case. Journal Name, 45(3), 201–215. https://doi.org/xxx
Chicago Notes-Bibliography (Humanities, History, Law)
Footnotes + bibliography; full author names; title case
Smith, John A., Barbara Jones, and Carl K. Williams. "Article Title." Journal Name 45, no. 3 (2021): 201–215. https://doi.org/xxx
MLA 9th Edition (Literature, Language Studies)
Author-page; Works Cited; containers concept
Smith, John A., et al. "Article Title." Journal Name, vol. 45, no. 3, 2021, pp. 201–215. JSTOR, https://doi.org/xxx
IEEE Style (Engineering, Computer Science)
Numbered in brackets; abbreviated journal titles; initials first
[1] J. A. Smith, B. Jones, and C. K. Williams, "Article title," IEEE Trans. Neural Netw. Learn. Syst., vol. 45, no. 3, pp. 201–215, Mar. 2021, doi: xxx
ACS Style (Chemistry, Chemical Engineering)
Numbered; abbreviated journal names in italics; year bold
Smith, J. A.; Jones, B.; Williams, C. K. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2021, 143, 1234–1240. DOI: 10.1021/xxx
AMA Style (JAMA, Medical journals)
Numbered; abbreviated journal names; all authors listed up to 6
Smith JA, Jones B, Williams CK, et al. Article title. JAMA. 2021;325(8):701-711. doi:10.1001/jama.xxx
📌
Pro tip: Reference management software (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) can automatically reformat an entire bibliography from one citation style to another. Always verify auto-formatted citations against official journal style guides.
About Scriptorium

The Art & Craft of Scholarly Transformation

The word scriptorium (Latin: scribere, to write) once described the sacred chambers of medieval monasteries where monks labored in candlelight, copying and illuminating manuscripts for the ages. Today, the act of scholarly writing carries no less weight — a doctoral thesis represents years of original thought demanding its rightful place in the world's literature.

Scriptorium was built for the scholar who has completed that arduous journey and now faces a different challenge: reshaping a 300-page dissertation into a focused, 4,000-word manuscript suited for a specific journal's exacting requirements. The knowledge is there — the translation is the art.

"A thesis proves you can do research. A manuscript proves you can communicate it."
— Academic publishing tradition

Every journal in this library has been documented with real submission guidelines, actual word limits, and specific structural requirements. The conversion tips are drawn from the accumulated wisdom of scholarly publishing — not generic advice, but targeted guidance for each publication's unique expectations.
